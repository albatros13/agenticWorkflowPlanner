# Care Process: Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia (BPH) and/or Prostate Cancer

## Functional definition

> Care process for patients affected by benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH) and/or prostate cancer (PCa) who:
>
> 1. Are referred from Primary Care to Specialist Care after meeting a set of inclusion criteria.
> 2. In Specialist Care, following a basic assessment (medical history including digital rectal examination, IPSS, urine sediment, uroflowmetry and PSA), receive a diagnosis, based on which the appropriate treatment and follow-up are established at the appropriate level of care.

---

## Quality standards — Diagnosis

1. When BPH or prostate cancer is suspected, the family physician should take a medical history in all cases, including:
   - Medical history/anamnesis.
   - IPSS (International Prostate Symptom Score).
   - Physical examination, including digital rectal examination.

2. At the first Primary Care consultation for suspected BPH or prostate cancer:
   - Urinalysis/urine sediment.
   - PSA, according to the established criteria.

3. Uroflowmetry should always be performed before assessment at the first urology consultation.

4. When BPH is confirmed at the first urological assessment, medical treatment should be prescribed:
   - Finasteride, or
   - Alpha-blockers.

5. Follow-up should be performed by the family physician:
   - After one month for alpha-blocker treatment.
   - After three months for finasteride.

6. At follow-up, the family physician should assess at least the medical history and IPSS.
   - **If treatment is found to be ineffective** — defined as less than a 25% improvement in IPSS — treatment should be discontinued and the patient referred back to the urologist.
   - **If treatment is effective** — a 25% improvement in IPSS — it should be continued, with annual follow-up including medical history, examination and IPSS.

7. If BPH requiring surgery is diagnosed at the first urological assessment, a transrectal ultrasound should be performed to measure prostate size and determine the appropriate surgical treatment.

8. If no diagnosis is reached at the first urological assessment, additional tests should be performed according to the relevant criteria:
   - **Comprehensive urological ultrasound** when associated symptoms/signs are present, such as pain, haematuria, recurrent infections or elevated creatinine.
   - **Pressure-flow study** when there is clinical/uroflowmetry discordance.
   - **Blood tests** including complete blood count and biochemistry, including renal and hepatic function, when appropriate.
   - **Ultrasound-guided transrectal prostate biopsy** when:
     - Digital rectal examination is abnormal.
     - Total PSA > 10.
     - Total PSA is between 3 and 10 with free PSA < 20%.

9. Surgical treatment for BPH:
   - Open surgery for large prostates.
   - Endoscopic surgery for small prostates.
   - Antibiotic and anticoagulation prophylaxis should always be provided.
   - Urological review after surgery should occur no later than three months after discharge. Possible complications should be assessed and, if recovery is satisfactory, the patient is discharged definitively from the care process.

10. If, based on the medical history and complementary investigations, the urologist diagnoses prostate cancer (positive biopsy), staging investigations should be requested:
    - Chest X-ray, PA and lateral views.
    - Bone scan when:
      - PSA > 10;
      - Gleason score > 7;
      - Seminal-vesicle biopsy is positive;
      - There are symptoms of bone pain.

11. After staging, treatment of prostate cancer should be initiated, taking into account that:
    - **Radiotherapy:** should begin no later than two months after the indication is established.
    - **Surgery:** should be performed no later than two months after the indication.
    - **Hormonal blockade:** should be initiated on the same day.
    - **Urological follow-up:**
      - Within one month after surgery, including PSA assessment.
      - Within three months after radiotherapy, including assessment of PSA, clinical status and possible adverse effects.
      - Every six months during hormonal blockade, with possible complications such as gynecomastia and hepatic dysfunction monitored by the family physician.

## Quality standards included in the 2002 SAS Programme Contract

- The IPSS (International Prostate Symptom Score–Quality of Life) should be applied during the initial Primary Care assessment of all patients presenting with prostatic symptoms.
- PSA testing should follow the criteria defined in the care process.
- Prostate biopsies should be ultrasound-guided and performed according to the established criteria.
- Diagnostic confirmation of patients referred to Specialist Care should occur within three months of the first Primary Care consultation for suspected prostate pathology.
- Bone scans in patients diagnosed with prostate cancer should be requested according to the criteria established in the care process.

## Process architecture — BPH treatment

The treatment pathway distinguishes **medical treatment**, **surgical treatment** and **alternative treatment**.

### Medical treatment

- Finasteride.
- Alpha-blockers.
- Temporary combined treatment with both.

Treatment is reassessed:

- Alpha-blockers: after 30 days.
- Finasteride: after three months.

### Surgical treatment

Considered according to prostate size and clinical characteristics:

| Prostate size | Procedure |
| --- | --- |
| < 30 g | TURP/related transurethral procedure |
| 30–60 g | Transurethral resection |
| 30–60 g with middle-lobe involvement | Appropriate transurethral procedure |
| > 60 g | Adenomectomy |

### Annual review

- Medical history.
- Physical examination.
- IPSS.

## Prostate cancer — diagnosis and staging

The diagnostic/staging pathway considers:

- Digital rectal examination.
- PSA and PSA-related measures.
- Prostate size.
- Biopsy results.
- Gleason score.
- Clinical findings.

Following confirmation of carcinoma, tumour staging includes:

- Chest X-ray.
- Bone scan.

The document then connects this diagnostic pathway to the treatment pathway.

---

## Prostate cancer — treatment

Treatment selection takes into account:

- Tumour stage.
- Degree of differentiation.
- Patient age.
- Life expectancy.
- PSA.
- Gleason score.
- Patient preferences.
- Symptoms/pain.

### T1–T2, N0, M0

1. Radical prostatectomy.
2. Radiotherapy.
3. Brachytherapy.
4. Surveillance in selected patients with life expectancy under 10 years.

### T3, N0, M0

1. Radiotherapy, with neoadjuvant and adjuvant hormonal therapy.
2. Hormonal treatment — orchiectomy or an LHRH agonist.
3. Radical prostatectomy in selected patients.
4. For lower urinary tract symptoms (LUTS): radiotherapy, hormonal treatment or palliative TURP.

### T4 N0 M0, or any T with N+ and/or M+

1. Hormonal treatment — orchiectomy or an LHRH agonist.
2. Radiotherapy in selected T4 N0 M0 patients, with neoadjuvant/adjuvant hormonal therapy.
3. For LUTS: palliative radiotherapy or palliative TURP.

The document states that treatment options are ordered according to the priority given by oncological criteria in the supporting bibliography.
